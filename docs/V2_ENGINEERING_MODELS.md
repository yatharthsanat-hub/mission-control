# MISSION CONTROL 2.0 engineering models

These are implementation specifications and candidate numerical fixtures. **A** marks sourced laws or published inputs, **B** an engineering approximation, **C** an illustrative game parameter or policy, and **D** planned capability without an implementation. These labels describe provenance. The separate verification status is tracked in [Scientific validation](SCIENTIFIC_VALIDATION.md). Phase 0 implements the Earth-only gravity and Kepler kernel and an illustrative agency ledger. Other models in this document are proposed. Values in worked cases are rounded for reading. Generate full-precision fixtures independently before setting release tolerances.

## 1. Newtonian gravitation and flight environment

**A/B.** Use body-relative inertial position **r** (m) and velocity **v** (m/s). `ṙ = v` and `v̇ = −μr/|r|³`, with `μ = GM` (m³/s²). During unforced arcs monitor specific energy `ε = |v|²/2 − μ/|r|` (J/kg) and angular momentum `h = r × v` (m²/s). In SI, DE440 gives Earth μ = 3.98600435507×10¹⁴ and Moon μ = 4.902800118×10¹². Bench case at Earth radius from center 7,000 km gives 8.134703 m/s² acceleration and circular speed 7,546.053 m/s. [JPL Astrodynamic Parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html)

**B/C scope.** Baseline accepts exterior bound ellipses with eccentricity ≤ 0.3, both apsis altitudes within 300–1,500 km at Earth or 50–500 km at Moon, at most 24 h per Earth run or 6 h per lunar science run. These are proposed product envelopes subject to verification, not accuracy bounds. Initial lunar state is supplied inside the envelope. Use one spherical body per coast, with illustrative sphere radii 6,371 km and 1,737.4 km pinned in the scenario. An Earth body-fixed preview may use a fixed spin axis, explicit initial rotation and constant sidereal rotation. Use versioned SPICE orientation and state samples for lunar survey geometry within their covered interval. Store source frame, body origin and time scale. Use TDB seconds past J2000 for ephemeris lookup, elapsed seconds for event execution and a tested conversion for UTC display. For rotating frame velocity transforms include the rotation derivative. Reject body intersection by apsis or event detection, and report unsupported conditions. Atmospheric drag, oblateness, lunar mascons and third-body perturbations require a more capable model, especially for longer forecasts. [NAIF Frames](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/toolkit_docs/C/req/frames.html) · [NAIF Time](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/toolkit_docs/C/req/time.html) · [ESA Types of orbits](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/space-transportation/types-of-orbits)

## 2. Kepler elements, propagation and visibility

**A/B.** Require an epoch and inertial axes for `a` (semimajor axis, m), `e` (eccentricity), `i` (inclination), `Ω` (ascending-node longitude), `ω` (periapsis argument) and `M₀` (mean anomaly), with angles in rad. Compute `n = √(μ/a³)` (rad/s), `M = M₀ + nΔt`, and solve `E − e sin E = M` for eccentric anomaly E using a bounded bracket and Newton steps with bisection fallback. Use consistent anomaly wrapping. Perifocal position is `a(cos E−e, √(1−e²)sin E, 0)`; velocity is `an/(1−e cos E) (−sin E, √(1−e²)cos E, 0)`. Rotate both by active right-handed `Rz(Ω) Rx(i) Rz(ω)`. Period `T=2π/n`. Store Cartesian state after maneuvers. For singular display elements use argument of latitude, longitude of periapsis or true longitude as appropriate to the frame, and mark undefined elements. Bench cases: 7,000 km circular Earth period 5,828.5167 s; `a=8,000 km,e=0.125` initialized at periapsis reaches 9,000 km with speed 6,225.176 m/s at 3,560.5408 s; 100 km circular lunar orbit using the scenario sphere has period 7,067.4597 s. The 8,000 km ellipse is an analytic solver fixture outside the release Earth apogee envelope. [Weber, Circular and Elliptical Orbits](https://orbital-mechanics.space/time-since-periapsis-and-keplers-equation/elliptical-orbits.html)

**B.** Cylindrical shadow from a sphere of radius R (m) applies when `r·ŝ < 0` and `|r − (r·ŝ)ŝ| < R`, with unit Sun direction ŝ from body to Sun. Ground elevation is `asin(û·ρ̂)` with û local up, **ρ** station-to-craft displacement (m) and ρ̂ its unit vector in the same frame. Check elevation ≥ the terminal mask and a clear segment for supported occulting spheres. Test the minimum distance over the **finite segment**, allowing a station/target surface endpoint when the line points outward. For observation, apply the same visibility geometry to the target, then require local illumination and sensor constraints. Bench case: at zero Sun beta angle the circular 7,000 km Earth fixture has eclipse duration `2 asin(R/r)/n ≈ 2,121.745 s`. A station directly under the craft has 90° elevation. Bracket event transitions and search for minima/grazing windows so a coarse step cannot silently skip supported events. Penumbra, atmospheric refraction, local terrain masking and high-precision Earth orientation are omitted. Ephemerides are offline versioned inputs. [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html)

## 3. Propulsion and delta-v

**A/B.** For one supported constant-thrust vacuum engine, `vₑ=g₀Isp`, `Δv=vₑ ln(m₀/m₁)`, `m_used=m₀[1−exp(−|Δv|/vₑ)]`, `ṁ=F/vₑ`, and burn duration estimate `m_used/ṁ`. F is thrust (N), mass is kg, Isp is s, delta-v and exhaust speed are m/s, and standard gravity g₀ is 9.80665 m/s². At the impulse, keep position continuous and apply the inertial velocity vector and fuel debit together. Reject a fuel shortfall, incompatible propellant, a thrust direction outside pointing authority, or a predicted burn duration exceeding 1% of local orbital period for the baseline impulse feature. Reserve mass remains unavailable unless the contract explicitly permits releasing it. Bench case: 100 to 80 kg at 300 s gives 656.487 m/s. For a coplanar circular transfer, `aₜ=(r₁+r₂)/2`, `v(r)=√(μ(2/r−1/aₜ))`; take the velocity differences at the two apsides and `t_transfer=π√(aₜ³/μ)`. The 7,000 to 8,000 km Earth fixture needs 247.477 and 239.347 m/s prograde and 3,232.011 s coast. Its second altitude is outside the first release envelope, so keep it at kernel level. Use the same mechanics for a tangential lunar circularization from the supplied arrival ellipse. Finite burns, ascent drag/gravity losses, staging, low thrust and interbody transfer optimization are deferred. [NASA Ideal Rocket Equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/ideal-rocket-equation/) · [Weber, Hohmann Transfer](https://orbital-mechanics.space/orbital-maneuvers/hohmann-transfer.html)

**C lunar fixture.** Use a Moon-centered ellipse with 100 km periapsis and 200 km apoapsis. A retrograde impulse at periapsis circularizes near 100 km. With a fictional wet mass of 40 kg, 220 s vacuum Isp and 20 N thrust, the estimates are 21.496 m/s, 0.39655 kg used and 42.78 s equivalent firing time. Compare to a finite-burn case before accepting this impulse approximation for a released contract. The fixture hardware values are synthetic.

## 4. Mass and center of mass

**A/B.** `m_total=Σmⱼ` and `r_COM=Σ(mⱼrⱼ)/m_total`. Each positive mass (kg) has its local centroid transformed into root axes (m). Dry components and remaining tank contents are separate terms. Use a fixed tank propellant centroid in each tank frame, bounded fill from empty to capacity, and explicit loaded/dry display states. Bench case: 4 kg at (0,0,0) m and 2 kg at (0.30,0,0) m give 6 kg at (0.10,0,0) m. Zero total mass, duplicate/invalid topology and unbounded geometry are input errors. Ignore slosh, flex and changing fluid centroid. Launch CoM check uses the launch interface frame. [NASA Center of Gravity](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/center-of-gravity/)

## 5. Solar generation and battery storage

**B/C.** `P_g = χ S Σ[Aᵢ ηᵢ max(0,n̂ᵢ·ŝ)]`. Eclipse factor χ is 0/1, irradiance S is W/m², area A is m², efficiency η lies within [0,1], and normals and Sun direction are unit vectors in the same frame. Bus demand `P_d` (W) comes from the active mode and enabled loads. Define surplus `P_s=max(P_g−P_d,0)` and deficit `P_f=max(P_d−P_g,0)`. Integrate energy E (J) over interval δt (s) at `dE/dt=η_c min(P_s,P_charge,max)−min(P_f,P_discharge,max)/η_d` where the caps are bus-side W and efficiencies are positive through 1. Split/clamp at 0 and `E_max`, ledger curtailed energy, conversion heat and unmet demand, and inhibit nonessential loads before recording an outage per mission policy. Declare initial energy in each manifest. Check simultaneous load, rate limit, geometric contact change and depletion boundaries. Bench case: 6 W surplus for 300 s at 90% charge efficiency adds 1,620 J below capacity; a 3 W deficit for 300 s at 90% discharge efficiency removes 1,000 J with sufficient charge and rate. Use constant efficiency and irradiance within an integration interval. Cell voltage, aging, temperature dependence and self-shadow of other parts are outside first scope. [NASA Small Spacecraft Technology · Power](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/power-subsystems/)

## 6. Thermal balance

**A/B/C.** One lumped isothermal node with `C_th dT/dt = Q_abs + Q_internal − εσA_rad(T⁴−T_sink⁴)`. T and effective sink temperature are K, `C_th=Σm c_p` is J/K, heat rates are W, A is m², ε is dimensionless and σ is 5.670374419×10⁻⁸ W·m⁻²·K⁻⁴. Contribute absorbed solar heat `α χ S A_projected` with absorptivity α. If a lump includes solar panels, subtract generated electrical power from its absorbed heat before adding dissipation where the power is consumed. Use a declared net term or separate heat ledger for battery losses, radiated RF and other exported power. Use positive thermal capacity, emissivity through 1, and an initial prototype screening domain of 150–400 K, with component-specific tighter operating limits and convergence checks. Bench case: 100 W constant net heating, ε=0.8, 1 m², sink 0 K yields 216.683 K equilibrium. Integrate with a stable bounded scheme and terminate/inhibit on configured boundaries. Single-node temperature cannot establish individual part survival. Earth IR/albedo, radiation view factors, thermal contacts and gradients need explicit parameters and separate validation for fidelity claims. [NASA Small Spacecraft Technology · Thermal Control](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/thermal-control/)

## 7. Communications and data return

**A/B.** On an eligible line of sight, `L_fs=20 log10(4πdf/c)` dB, with distance d (m), frequency f (Hz) and vacuum c (m/s). `P_rx=P_tx,dBW+G_tx,dBi+G_rx,dBi−L_fs−L_other`. `C/N₀=P_rx−10 log10(k_B T_sys)` in dB-Hz with T (K), k_B (J/K); `E_b/N₀=C/N₀−10 log10(R_b)` dB for bit rate (bit/s). Margin subtracts the selected mode's required `E_b/N₀` and design margin. Choose the highest eligible supported mode with nonnegative margin using mode-specific thresholds and a declared overhead factor. Use positive far-field range, frequency, temperature and bitrate, static losses and a band-matched terminal; a missing input yields unevaluable feasibility. At d=1,000 km and f=2.2 GHz, FSPL is 159.2962 dB. At d=384,400 km one-way delay is 1.2822 s. Integrate whole delivered bits over contact with a fractional remainder and a FIFO product ledger. Clamp to the queue, debit once and complete only at full product receipt. For lunar links test occultation on the spacecraft-to-station segment and use `d/c` as a first-order propagation delay, with encounter timing tolerances documented. Fixed loss allocation omits weather variability, detailed Doppler and real terminal scheduling. [ITU-R P.525](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.525/en) · [NASA Small Spacecraft Technology · Communications](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/soa-communications/) · [NASA Ground Data Systems](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/ground-data-systems-and-mission-operations/)

## 8. Attitude and pointing

**B/C.** Use a normalized quaternion q and finite rate-limited target orientation in the same inertial axes. For unit boresight b and target direction u, `θ=acos(clamp(b·u,−1,1))` (rad). A slew through principal quaternion angle `φ=2 acos(clamp(|q₁·q₂|,0,1))` has planning duration `t=φ/ω_max+t_settle` (s). Use shortest-path interpolation for the simplified slew and inhibit exposure/transmission while settling or when the pointing error exceeds the mode tolerance. Positive rate and explicit mode/boresight/roll conventions required. Bench case: 90° at 2°/s plus 5 s settle takes 50 s, and q and −q describe the same attitude. The rate cap is a capability approximation. Torque, wheel momentum/saturation, sensor noise and closed-loop stability are outside the baseline. [NASA Small Spacecraft Technology · Guidance, Navigation, and Control](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/guidance-navigation-and-control/)

## 9. Instrument performance and product quality

**B/C.** Nadir pinhole approximation `GSD ≈ H p/f` (m/pixel) and `swath ≈ 2H tan(FOV/2)` (m), with local altitude H, pixel pitch p and focal length f in m, and FOV in rad. For footprint, intersect boresight and edge rays with the spherical surface and mask to a scenario tile grid. Use the nadir GSD estimate only within a proposed 5° off-nadir limit, ≤10° full FOV and valid positive geometry. Enforce spectral bands, Sun incidence, pointing, mode duration and deadline per contract. Data size is `pixels × bands × bitsPerSample` plus metadata under the declared encoding. No credit for incomplete files, duplicates or products failing a quality gate. Bench cases: H=500 km and p/f=10 μrad yield 5 m/pixel. LROC’s published 10 μrad at 50 km gives 0.5 m/pixel as an external sampling scale check. This checks only the relationship, separate from flight image or system performance. Exclude cloud retrieval, SNR/MTF claims, absolute radiometry, terrain correction and mineral or water detection. [LROC instrument specifications](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/about/specs) · [NASA Landsat 9](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/landsat-9/)

## 10. Launch and mission economics

**B/C.** `massMargin=m_limit−m_wet` in kg. Transform stowed vertices into the provider frame and check axis bounds and CoM box in m, connector fit, epoch and injection-state limits. No generic rocket capacity can override a mission-specific interface. Financial estimate is `Σ quantityᵢ×USD rateᵢ + planning reserve`, with units matching each rate, rounded by line to integer cents. Quantities and commitments are nonnegative and at most the unspent balance. Bench case: 18 kg against 20 kg leaves 2 kg, and an envelope violation still blocks launch. The [product example](V2_PRODUCT_SPEC.md#usd-economy) requires $408,000 to reserve and spend against, with each actual charge settled once. No vendor prices are assumed current and no financial accuracy is inferred. Dynamic loads, vibration, packaging qualification, regulatory certification and probabilistic cost estimation are deferred. [NASA CubeSat 101, §4.1](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/nasa_csli_cubesat_101_508.pdf#page=48) · [NASA Cost Estimating Handbook](https://www.nasa.gov/ocfo/ppc-corner/nasa-cost-estimating-handbook-ceh/)
