# Phase 4 · Lunar reconnaissance and candidate site assessment

## Player workflow

1. Complete the Headquarters training contract and the pointing stability study. The study unlocks lunar planning and the optional fine-pointing unit. Headquarters offers a one-time illustrative $250,000 program grant after the study so an agency that has already committed an Earth mission can fund follow-up lunar work.
2. Assemble and save a vehicle with an imager, solar wing, battery and radio. Add the unlocked fine-pointing unit to increase allowable pointing from 25° to 40°, at the cost of its mass, power and procurement load.
3. In Mission Planning, select Lunar reconnaissance. Choose landing-site screening or regional context survey and one of the two fictional candidate tiles. The study preset is an 80 km circular equatorial orbit. Pin a plan, change the orbit or candidate, and compare opportunities, scene deliveries, GSD, power and cost. Approval locks the configuration and reserves agency funds.
4. Open Mission Control. The approved scenario begins **after assumed successful lunar orbit insertion**. Play, pause, step or jump to the next opportunity. Arm imaging, command an individual capture, and arm or manually initiate downlink. The common operations model adjudicates each action.
5. Finish the mission and open Science and Program Review. The Lunar Site Assessment compares the current result with the latest completed saved runs for other sites. Export a result for later use. Acquiring scenes without completing downlink leaves the assessment incomplete.

### Frames, integration and limits

The lunar inertial frame is `moon-centered-synthetic-inertial-1`. The Moon-fixed frame is `moon-fixed-synthetic-mean-spin-1` with planetocentric signed latitude and east-positive longitude in degrees. Simulation time is elapsed TDB seconds from a zero J2000 epoch field with deliberately synthetic axes and orientation. The frame is not a SPICE or IAU orientation product. The two-body trajectory supports circular and eccentric orbits with both apsides 60–400 km above the reference sphere. GMAT, SPICE and lunar gravity harmonics are outside this implementation. Mascons, third-body terms, libration, terrain and Earth-caused solar eclipses are **NOT IMPLEMENTED**.

Forecast and flight both consume `createFlightModel`. The approved frame selects gravity in the shared solver and the environment supplies radius, rotation, sunlight and the contact strategy. Time integration is 5 s with midpoint sampling for 24 hours. Short windows may be missed. Imaging and radio compete for one activity slot. Science is FIFO and only fully returned scenes receive credit. Orbital trace samples and site markers use the same fixed frame as the 2D ground track. Moon surface shading is procedural, and the green target marker and footprint readout track actual modeled acquisitions.

## Assessment and saves

- **Approval schema 3** freezes the lunar environment, scenario, body/frame identifiers, optics, initial orbit, research authorization, launch service, forecast assumptions and provenance. Earth schemas 1 and 2 keep their original structure and rules. Legacy schema 1 still requires reapproval to enter operations.
- **Agency save schema 5** stores immutable approvals and command checkpoints. Lunar ledger commitments identify the destination, and checkpoints attach only to a matching executable approval. Successful launch review reserves $31,000 for the fictional post-insertion service, $12,000 for operations and the actual assembly procurement cost. A 4.77 kg example with the fine-pointing unit commits $125,700. The envelope and limit come from the shared extended launch scenario. The grant is explicitly claimed and checked once per research-qualified agency. Running, completing, replaying and resetting add no payments or awards.
- **LunarSiteAssessment schema 1** is recovered from a finished saved run via `readLunarSiteAssessment(mission, record)`. The approved snapshot and persisted command checkpoint are the source of truth. The exported JSON includes mission ID, site ID and coordinates, frame, eligible windows, acquired and complete delivered scenes, required scenes, per-scene coverage dimensions and sampling and lighting, modeled orbital Earth access window count, confidence, unknowns and source/model provenance. The current review compares site evidence side by side. Measurements describe repeat local optical context at the modeled time, with no union of overlapping footprints.
- **Interpretation.** The comparison labels evidence as unobserved, incomplete or optical context returned, depending on the saved data. Every result leaves landing safety **NOT ASSESSED**. Slope, local hazards, persistent lighting, subsurface water, thermal survival, surface bearing strength and surface radio logistics are outside the supplied instrument and environment. The named tiles are fictional training areas, with no operational landing designation.

The Earth and Moon workflow and assessment contract provide a starting point for Phase 5 lunar logistics. Surface mechanics, terrain and hazard inputs, operational communications, descent and landing remain outside Phase 4.
