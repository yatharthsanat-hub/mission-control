# Phase 1 · Vehicle Assembly Building

Open **Vehicle Assembly** from an agency’s headquarters. Select a catalog part, choose a compatible mount or click its marker, inspect the ghost, then attach. The part inspector rotates, detaches or removes the selection. Removing a part also removes its descendants. Detached subassemblies retain their instance IDs and can be reattached from their root. Save the draft explicitly. Each accepted edit, undo, or redo creates a new immutable revision. Undo and redo cover up to 50 recent steps in the open session. The saved draft persists independently of this history.

The Three.js viewport uses React Three Fiber and Drei orbit controls. It draws the engine’s resolved poses and center of mass, and supports orbit, zoom, pan, fit, axes and envelope overlays. Catalog, mount, placement and inspection actions also work by keyboard and touch without WebGL. Viewport motion is direct manipulation with damping disabled. The 3D module loads on demand.

## Saves and research

IndexedDB stores the active agency, one recovery revision and a list of archived raw snapshots. Headquarters can list, export and explicitly restore archives. Restore archives the displaced active snapshot when its contents are new to the archive. Equivalent snapshots ignore the agency-save ID and storage revision when checking for duplicates. A duplicate import leaves the active record untouched. Transactional comparison rejects stale writers.

Phase 0 schemas 1 and 2 still load. Schema 3 adds a deterministic, catalog-versioned assembly snapshot to the agency envelope. Reopening the same source catalog reproduces the placements and readouts. Saved parts requiring the `fine-pointing-adcs` capability are accepted only when that agency’s research history unlocks it. Assembly procurement is a projected cost. It does not post a payment or financial commitment.

## Engineering and NASA provenance

The fixed bus and its rooted mounted tree use typed attachment interfaces and 90° twist increments. The engine composes parent pose, host-node pose, the mating transform and inverse guest-node pose. The opposing port normals meet at the shared mount. All transforms are exact signed-axis permutations with SI-metre translations. Rectangular stowed bodies can touch at a face. Intersecting interiors block the edit. The launch envelope, wet-mass ceiling and bus-origin center-of-mass offsets produce separate fit checks. Out-of-envelope drafts remain editable and saveable with the affected parts flagged.

Dry and wet mass sum the same catalog kg because this release contains no propulsion or tank model. The center of mass is the mass-weighted mean of transformed local centroids. Bounds combine transformed per-part boxes. Nominal solar watts, usable battery joules, simultaneous peak load watts, and procurement USD cents are summed independently. These are preliminary assembly estimates. Solar output in an actual mission depends on attitude, exposure and eclipses. Thermal limits, harnesses, structural loads, propulsion, RF budgets, detailed pointing and science return are outside this workspace. Box geometry and discrete rotations exclude arbitrary shapes, articulation and flexible parts.

- The stowed-envelope and center-of-mass checks apply the principle of evaluating the spacecraft against a mission-specific interface. The offer, limits and mount dimensions are illustrative. [NASA CubeSat 101, §4.1 and §§6.4–6.6](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/nasa_csli_cubesat_101_508.pdf)
- The array, battery, radio and attitude-control roles follow published subsystem guidance. The part specifications are representative game inputs. [NASA Small Spacecraft Technology, Power](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/power-subsystems/) · [Communications](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/soa-communications/) · [Guidance, Navigation and Control](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/guidance-navigation-and-control/)
- The imager represents a red and near-infrared observation concept. The separate Phase 0 training contract uses published band ranges. Assembly does not model an OLI camera or infer crop condition. [NASA Landsat 9 OLI](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/landsat-9/)

## Verification evidence

Assembly transforms, bounds and mass accounting are **TESTED** against independent hand calculations recorded in [assembly.test.ts](../tests/assembly.test.ts). The bus, camera rotated 90°, and side-mounted wing total 3.200 kg. The CoM is approximately (0.01339844, 0.00531250, 0.05246094) m and bounds measure (0.245, 0.220, 0.375) m. A camera on a riser reaches 0.340 m on +Z against a 0.310 m limit while remaining within mass. Tests also cover collisions, cycles, occupied and incompatible nodes, invalid inputs, research locks, history, round trips, archive deduplication and stale writes. Catalog, costs, nominal power estimates and launch offer are **ILLUSTRATIVE**. They carry no flight-qualification claim.

The 26 automated tests include all 12 original tutorial regressions and all 8 Phase 0 tests. Strict TypeScript checks for both configs, ESLint and the production build passed. Chrome verified WebGL part selection and camera orbit, mount filtering, collision blocking, keyboard rotation, history, draft reload, research gating, and archive export and confirmed restore. A touch viewport with WebGL deliberately disabled verified attachment through the fallback. The classic tutorial reached mission success in the same browser check.

The production build warns about the on-demand 3D bundle size (approximately 248 kB gzip). The dependency audit reported seven moderate entries in the development lint dependency tree. Hardware GPU performance and browsers outside Chrome remain unverified.
