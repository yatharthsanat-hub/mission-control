# Phase 2 · Earth-observation mission planning

## Player workflow

1. Establish or open an agency in Headquarters. In Vehicle Assembly, mount an imager, solar wing, battery, and radio, then **Save draft**. Additional hardware can affect fit, load, research eligibility, and funding.
2. Open **Mission Planning**. Select the current saved assembly or the immutable spacecraft snapshot from an approved mission. Select the science objective, target, launch service, and six orbital elements.
3. Review the sampled ground track, target coordinates, orbital period and apsides, service fit, payload bands, power, battery, radio, and itemized USD costs. PASS applies to the stated check. A FAIL blocks approval and identifies an edit. WARNING records an unresolved feasibility question that follows the mission into the handoff.
4. **Approve and commit funds** freezes the configuration and reserves the exact hardware, service, and operations total from available cash. It records a commitment in Headquarters. It does not record an expense or perform a launch.
5. Reload to see approved plans, export a standalone mission JSON, or revise an approved plan. Revision starts with its frozen spacecraft and inputs. Select the latest saved assembly to use updated hardware. Each approved revision has a new ID and its own commitment.

The standard launch profiles, procurement costs, USD allowances, camera, radio, storage, targets, and terminal are illustrative. One Earth-observation training contract identifier ties the planner to the program. The Phase 0 concept review and research progression remain available in Headquarters. The planner can be used before that review settles if the agency has enough uncommitted cash. The Phase 1 single active assembly draft per agency is preserved. Multiple approved configurations and their immutable spacecraft snapshots can be selected. The local portfolio is capped at eight approvals per agency. Releasing, paying, or closing planning commitments belongs to a later phase.

## MissionConfiguration v1

The Phase 3 consumer starts with `restoreMission(JSON.parse(text))` from `src/v2/planning/mission.ts`, or reads `save.missions` after `decodeSave`. Both return a deeply frozen configuration. The planner persists agency save schema 4, which still reads older schemas through the existing migration path. IndexedDB compares the expected active record and atomically writes the new snapshot and ledger commitment. Import and archive restore can change the local save ID. The captured agency reference remains the original approval reference.

| Contract field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `schemaVersion`, `id`, `contractId`, `approval`, `plan.revisedFrom` | Version and immutable mission, account, training contract, approval day, pre-approval cash, unlocked capabilities, and optional prior-plan reference. |
| `catalogRevision`, `assembly`, `spacecraft` | Deep snapshot of parts and joints, resolved mount poses and bounds, masses, center of mass, loads, generation, battery, camera bands and scene encoding, storage and radio rate. Resolution comes from the Phase 1 assembly engine. |
| `orbit.elements`, `orbit.initialState`, `units` | SI orbital elements and Cartesian position, velocity and acceleration in Earth-centered J2000 axes at the stated TDB epoch. UI degrees and km are converted before propagation. The start epoch is zero TDB seconds since J2000. |
| `objective`, `target`, `environment`, `executionInputs` | Required spectral intervals, scene goal, ideal sample spacing and solar elevation thresholds, spherical target center and radius, fixed Sun, station threshold and cap, start resources, 60 s cadence, six-hour duration, and named versioned geometry and policy assumptions. |
| `budget`, `launch`, `findings` | Integer USD cents, service limits in the assembly root frame, and an audit trail of readiness and remaining warnings at approval. |
| `models`, `earthGravity`, `sources` | Scenario, Earth model, environment and source registry revisions, published source input and SI conversion, and source locators supporting the review. |

Runtime restoration checks the input shape and known model/catalog, then rebuilds the scenario and readiness from the captured inputs. It rejects mismatched thresholds, fields, findings, spacecraft properties, missing approvals and inconsistent reservations. To account for cross-runtime trigonometric rounding, it permits absolute component error of 10⁻⁷ m, 10⁻¹⁰ m/s and 10⁻¹² m/s² on the Cartesian position, velocity and acceleration respectively, and retains the original approved vectors. This validation is for corruption and consistency. It is not an authentication mechanism. Phase 3 can use the saved SI elements with `propagate` and the stored model revisions. It must not read the mutable assembly draft or infer parameters from labels.

## Scientific scope and execution assumptions

- **TESTED · Earth two-body propagation.** Central Earth gravity, elliptic inputs with both apsides between 300 and 1,500 km above the 6,371 km scenario sphere. The kernel also caps eccentricity at 0.3 and propagation at one day. The planner computes apsides as `a(1 ± e) − R` and gets period and state from the inherited kernel. There is no drag, oblateness, maneuver, real ephemeris, Earth orientation product, or operational orbit prediction. Independent Phase 0 analytical and Cartesian-integration cases remain. The external GMAT case remains pending.
- **ILLUSTRATIVE · Spherical geography.** For elapsed TDB time from the scenario start, take inertial longitude `atan2(y, x)`, subtract `2πt / siderealDay`, and wrap to [−180°, 180°). Latitude is `atan2(z, hypot(x, y))`. Synthetic meridian angle is 0 rad at the start. Geographic coordinates use signed degrees, north/east positive. The phase does not convert to UTC or align with observed Earth at J2000. The displayed subpoints are spaced 60 s apart and do not demonstrate a target pass, footprint, or link.
- **ILLUSTRATIVE · Planning screening.** Camera coverage is interval containment. Ideal nadir ground sample distance is altitude × pixel IFOV in metres, with the small-angle assumption. Perigee gives the optimistic lower bound. Solar nameplate output is compared with housekeeping and maximum concurrent load. Battery and communications checks establish the installed resources only. Thermal, attitude accuracy, link margin, orbit insertion, and real launch cost are outside the checks.
- **NOT IMPLEMENTED · Mission execution.** The configuration carries the inputs for a deterministic first executor. For `spherical-nadir-target-radius-1`, sample at the start of each 60 s interval. Compare the spherical arc distance from the subpoint to the target center with the target radius, compute ideal ground sample distance from current altitude, and test solar elevation from the outward target normal dotted with the fixed Sun direction. There is one scene per contiguous qualifying window and only a complete returned scene counts. No clouds or physically rendered observations are implied.
- **NOT IMPLEMENTED · Eclipse, contact and operations.** For the declared cylindrical shadow, eclipse requires a negative position projection on the Sun direction and perpendicular distance below the sphere radius. For the declared terminal model, rotate the station from body-fixed to inertial at each sample and compare the spacecraft line-of-sight elevation to its elevation mask. If eligible, use the lesser of the terminal and radio caps. The declared ideal battery model starts at the provided charge fraction, uses nominal generation in sunlight and zero in eclipse, and charges/discharges at unity efficiency subject to capacity. Count housekeeping in every interval and add the relevant payload, attitude (when imaging), or radio load. Attempt downlink of previously stored data first, then imaging if downlink is ineligible or cannot be powered. Keep partially transmitted scenes in FIFO order, consume storage for remaining onboard bits, and count complete scenes only. Carry blocked activities and any unserved housekeeping as telemetry rather than synthesizing science return. No execution model or mission closeout has been implemented here.

This phase follows the requested planning-only scope. The broader Phase 2 simulation and closeout gate in the original migration plan is deferred to the execution phase. Ground-track, energy, and data-handling assumptions need independent validity evidence before stronger claims.

## Verification

- `npm test` covers all 26 inherited tests and seven Phase 2 tests. Independent hand cases verify a 7,000 km semimajor axis with eccentricity 0.02 gives 489 km perigee and 769 km apogee, a period of about 5,828.517 s, and a 90° westward rotation of a fixed equatorial vector after one quarter of a sidereal day. Additional checks cover spectral selection, incompatible spacecraft and launch conditions, exact funding, schema tampering, freezing, stale approvals, and preservation after an assembly edit.
- TypeScript strict checks for the app and Node/test projects, ESLint, and production build pass. Vite continues to report the existing large lazy WebGL assembly chunk.
- Chrome walkthrough exercised agency creation, 3D assembly and save, the flood spectral blocker, service inclination checks and switching, keyboard approval, JSON export validated in Node, saved revision after reload, and the exact $105,200.00 commitment in Headquarters. The 360 px viewport showed no horizontal page overflow. The walkthrough reported no uncaught page errors.
